Work #1

Sound Composition "Untitled"

A 3 minute long sound piece originated from experiments in the ScratchBuilt Sound Lab.

Ambivalent on the continuity-discontinuity scale, with long progressive continuous passages of processed, mostly unrecognisable sources punctuated by three radical impacts. The structure moves from an abstract world through a brief recognisable one and back again, ending in rising intensity without resolution.

Analysis

Continuities, Discontinuities and Semantics

  1. 00:00

    Usage of elements like oboes, clarinets, processed caster chairs, drones and pitched down aircrafts create and ambience full of no clearly identifiable sources (environmental semantics with inconclusive causality). The piece starts with the oboes, clarinets and the processed caster chairs, that have changes in intensity throughout time. At 00:20 the drone and aircrafts are introduced and the oboes and processed caster chairs fade out. They keep growing in intensity.

  2. 00:38

    A large impact cuts the growing drone and aircrafts. It is not as disruptive as it seems, because we are growing in intensity, its occurrence and timing is expected. The impacts allows a transition of environment.

  3. 00:40

    Elements like leaves, wind, train horn, rails and bells are immediately identifiable (informational semantics), and the listener decodes a real-world scene (causal perception). From 01:10 the train gradually drops about ten semitones until it disappears at 01:20. At the same time the train fades out and is pitched down, enter gradually metal sheet effects (progressive continuity). These elements allow the audience to detach sound from cause and to slide into abstraction once again without rupture (sonic elongation).

  4. 01:20

    The metal sheet effects are joined by processed caster chairs (01:24) and oboes (01:34), that lead back to the opening environment. The intensity of these elements starts to drop. The same impact from 00:38 enters again at 01:48, this time more disruptive as its context is drastically different, here instead of the sound being even more low intensity it comes to break our expectations (radical discontinuity). It is followed by 8 seconds of silence.

  5. 01:50

    The processed caster chairs return as a slowly building rumble (progressive), at 02:02 it is followed by small percussive impacts. At 02:13 instead of another small percussive impact we hear the same impact from 00:38 and 01:48, this time it is again expected from the audience so it honest have the same intensity as the one before. The radical discontinuity is the transition from these elements to the next ones.

  6. 02:17

    Rising of musical material from the start of the sound piece, oboes and clarinets, now also joined by cellos and other instruments. The audience’s attention is guided toward sudden events (expressive semantics), while the sustained layers keep an environmental quality. A micropause at 02:30 and then a triangle at 02:31 restart the music. From 03:02 raw percussive impacts join in, dense and disjunctive composition. An unresolved oboe and cello crescendo closes the sound piece at 03:25.

Reflection

Overall, the piece sits at the ambivalent point of the scale. It's progressive continuity is interrupted by three radical impacts (00:38, 01:42, 02:13). In the continuous passages the listener chooses what to process, and the impacts take that control away. The recurring impact acts as a semi-activated memory, each return is anticipated unconsciously, then confirmed or denied. Many of the progressive builds prepare these ruptures, so continuity and discontinuity are interdependent rather than opposed. The piece ends in rising intensity, without resolution.
