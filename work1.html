Work #1

Sound Composition "Untitled"

A 3 minute long sound piece originated from experiments in the ScratchBuilt Sound Lab.

Ambivalent on the continuity-discontinuity scale, with long continuous passages (steady and progressive) of processed, mostly unrecognisable sources punctuated by three radical impacts. The structure moves from an abstract world through a brief recognisable one and back again, ending in rising intensity without resolution.

Analysis

Continuities and discontinuities

  1. 00:00

    Usage of no clearly identifiable sources, oboes and processed chair wheels, a drone and an aircraft. Steady to ambivalent continuity, with endogenous attention.

  2. 00:38

    A large impact cuts the growing drone. The build creates an expectation of continuation, and the impact reptures it. Radical discontinuity, with exogenous attention.

  3. 00:40

    Elements like leaves, wind, train horn, rails and bells form a long low-intensity passage. From 01:10 the train gradually drops about ten semitones until it disappears at 01:20. Progressive continuity, with no rupture, these elements slide into abstraction.

  4. 01:20

    Elements like metal sheets, chair wheels (01:24) and oboe (01:34) lead back to the opening environment, with ambivalent continuity. The opening impact returns at (01:42), followed by silence, the second radical discontinuity.

  5. 01:50

    The chair wheels return as a slowly building rumble (progressive), followed by rhythmic impacts from 02:02. The large impact at 02:13 closes the sequence, the third radical discontinuity.

  6. 02:17

    Rising musical material from the start of the sound piece, oboes, clarinets, cellos. A micropause (02:30) and then a triangle (02:31) restarts the music. From 03:02 raw impacts join in, dense and disjunctive composition, ambivalent. The crescendo ends at 03:25 unresolved.

Semantic shifts

  1. 00:00

    Environmental semantics with inconclusive causality. The ambiguous sources keep attention under the listener’s control and invite reduced listening.

  2. 00:40

    Informational semantics. Leaves, wind, train horn, rails and bell are immediately identifiable, and the listener decodes a real-world scene (causal perception).

  3. 01:10

    The gradual pitch drop of the train and metal sheet detach sound from cause. The only recognisable source is defamiliarised without rupture (sonic elongation), and listening returns to reduced perception.

  4. 02:17

    Instrumental and musical codes take over. Semantics become expressive and attention is guided toward sudden events, while the sustained layers keep an environmental quality.

Reflection

Overall, the piece sits at the ambivalent point of the scale. It combines steady and progressive continuity, and three radical impacts (00:38, 01:42, 02:13) interrupt them. In the continuous passages the listener chooses what to process, and the impacts take that control away. The recurring impact acts as a semi-activated memory, each return is anticipated unconsciously, then confirmed or violated. Many of the progressive builds prepare these ruptures, so continuity and discontinuity are interdependent rather than opposed. The piece ends in rising intensity, without resolution.
